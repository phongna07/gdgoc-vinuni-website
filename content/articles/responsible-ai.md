AI is now part of daily life, from virtual assistants to complex automation. Its rapid growth also raises hard questions about ethics, accountability and sustainability. That is why **Responsible AI** was the theme of GDGoC Hackathon Vietnam 2025.

Responsible AI is a set of principles and practices that make sure AI is built and used ethically, transparently, and for the benefit of society. It comes down to six areas.

## 1. Fairness and bias mitigation

Make sure training data is diverse, remove bias where you find it, and use fairness metrics to check.

## 2. Transparency and explainability

Users need to understand how an AI system reaches a decision and where its limits are. Take a company that spends 500 million VND on advertising because a model predicts that 80% of young customers will buy. Only 40% do, and the company loses 200 million. Had the team understood that the model relied on short-term data, they could have run a small test first.

## 3. Accountability and governance

Someone has to be responsible: a team that monitors risk, an ethics review process, and a way for users to give feedback.

## 4. Privacy and security

Follow data protection law, encrypt user data, and prevent unauthorised access.

## 5. Reliability and safety

Test the system in real conditions, have fallbacks, and monitor it continuously.

## 6. Inclusiveness and accessibility

AI should serve everyone, including people with disabilities, and should account for culture and language.

## With limited time

A hackathon gives you little time and few resources. Prioritise the core aspects and make design decisions you can defend: fair data, transparent behaviour, clear ownership, protected information, a safe system, and a product that works for everyone.
